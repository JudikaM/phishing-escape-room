Every phishing scenario in the escape room trains you to spot one or more of these 8 core red flag categories:

FAKE DOMAIN

Lookalike or unverified domains crafted to impersonate trusted companies and capture logins.

URGENCY

Artificial deadlines and threats of account deletion designed to rush you into acting without checking.

Requests for one-time passcodes, passwords, national IDs, or bank account numbers over email or chat.

// 04 · emergency recovery protocol

WHAT TO DO IF YOU FELL FOR IT

Falling for a scam happens to everyday users and security pros alike. If you clicked a malicious link, sent a verification code, or entered financial details, take these immediate, region-neutral response steps:

// step 01 · disconnect

STOP REPLYING IMMEDIATELY

Cut all communications with the scammer. Do not answer follow-up calls, emails, or chat messages, and do not attempt to negotiate.
// step 02 · official channel

CONTACT THE REAL SERVICE

Reach out to your bank, courier, or platform through their official app, saved browser bookmark, or the customer service number on the back of your payment card.
// step 03 · credential security

CHANGE PASSWORDS & ENABLE 2FA

Update passwords immediately for compromised accounts and any other services sharing that password. Enable two-factor authentication, preferably using an authenticator app.
// step 04 · fraud audit

CHECK RECENT ACTIVITY

Inspect bank statements, card charges, login sessions, and email forwarding rules. Request a temporary freeze on payment cards or credit files if unauthorized charges appear.
// step 05 · incident reporting

REPORT TO SERVICE & AUTHORITIES

Report the scam to the impersonated company fraud department and file an official report with your regional or national consumer protection / cybercrime agency.
// step 06 · alert your network

WARN FRIENDS & COWORKERS

If your email, messaging profile, or address book was compromised, alert your contacts promptly so attackers cannot impersonate you to target your colleagues or loved ones.
